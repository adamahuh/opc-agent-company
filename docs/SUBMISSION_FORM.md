## Field 3 — Full description

> **The problem (measured, not assumed):** Impact-driven businesses — weavers, restorers, environmental groups — are increasingly the *subject* of other people's pages while absent from the answers themselves. We measured it: some sites have infrastructure but no machine-readable meaning; some have broken crawl guidance (a real Singapore beadwork site's robots.txt and sitemap return HTTP 500 while its homepage stays 200 — reproduced from two machines, two networks); some practitioners have no owned site at all, existing only through press coverage.
>
> **The product:** embold'r, a one-person agency run on QwenWork agents. A free gated impact check (paste a URL: the impact gate *declines* non-impact businesses in software, with the refusal logged) runs seven machine-readability checks — including the site's stance toward the AI answer engines' crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, Google-Extended) across Google, Bing (which feeds ChatGPT and Copilot), and Yandex — then produces a plain-language letter to the practitioner with one-click, copy-paste fixes. Above the free tier sits a service ladder: a fixed-scope "AI Visibility Rescue" and an institutional annual account with scheduled re-measurement and plain-language briefings.
>
> **The discipline that makes it defensible:** every claim is dated, reproducible, and graded against Google's primary documentation. Refusals are enforced as data, not prose: ≤10 assets per engagement, practitioner sign-off before anything ships under a real name, no bulk AI pages, no YMYL claims, and no ranking promises — ever. Within 24 hours of publishing our own site, Anthropic's and OpenAI's crawlers read its robots.txt — the same file class that fails on our measured subject. We report discovery, not citation, because that is what the evidence shows.
>
> **The stack:** the customer-facing site is built with Hashmeta AI (its platform also records the AI-bot traffic evidence); the impact-check engine runs as a Node.js service with a Supabase registry; QwenWork is the engine throughout — sub-agents for research fan-out, a persistent marketing agent producing the video script and assets, scheduled tasks, live publishing, browser-verified UI screening, and an approval gate before anything outbound ships. One person, ~$0 marginal cost, the rigor an agency can't afford at this client size.
